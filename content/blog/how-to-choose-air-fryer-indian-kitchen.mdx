    - id: how-it-works
      title: How an Air Fryer Works
      icon: "⚡"
      image: "https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1626082927389-6cd097cdc6ec?w=800&q=80&auto=format"
      content: |
        An air fryer is a compact convection oven with a very powerful fan. It circulates extremely hot air at high speed around your food — creating the crispy exterior you get from deep frying, but using a thin coating of oil or none at all.

        The key difference from a regular oven is fan speed and chamber size. The hot air moves faster and the cooking space is smaller, so food crisps much more effectively than in a full-sized oven.

        For Indian cooking this matters because our snacks — samosas, pakoras, bread rolls, vada — traditionally get their crunch from hot oil. An air fryer replicates roughly 80% of that texture using 95% less oil. Not identical to deep frying, but for everyday cooking it is genuinely close enough — and your kitchen stays oil-splatter-free.

    - id: capacity
      title: Choosing the Right Capacity
      icon: "📏"
      image: "https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1484723091739-30a097e8f929?w=800&q=80&auto=format"
      content: |
        Capacity is the most common mistake Indian buyers make. Almost everyone buys too small.

        The simple rule: multiply the number of people you cook for by 1.5 litres. A family of 4 needs at least 5 to 6 litres.

        **1.5 to 2 litres** — Single person or couple. Can do 4 to 6 samosas at a time. Fine for light snacks and reheating only.

        **3 to 4 litres** — Small family of 3. Handles a reasonable batch of pakoras. Will feel limiting during festivals or when guests arrive.

        **5 to 6 litres** — Family of 4 to 5. The sweet spot for Indian households. Can handle a whole chicken, large snack batches, or a full tray in one go.

        **7 litres and above** — Large families or people who entertain frequently. Takes more counter space and costs more. Only worth it if you regularly cook for 6 or more people.

    - id: wattage
      title: Wattage and Indian Voltage Conditions
      icon: "🔌"
      image: "https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1509395176047-4a66953fd231?w=800&q=80&auto=format"
      content: |
        Indian households run on 220-240V supply. All air fryers sold in India are compatible, but wattage directly affects cooking performance.

        **Below 1200W** — Avoid. Cook times become very long, results are uneven, and you lose the crispy texture that makes air frying worthwhile.

        **1200W to 1400W** — Acceptable for reheating and simple dishes. Not ideal for authentic Indian frying results.

        **1400W to 1600W** — The recommended range. Heats quickly, maintains temperature well, produces good crisping on samosas and pakoras.

        **1600W and above** — Excellent. Faster preheat, better crisping, handles larger batches evenly. Worth the slightly higher electricity consumption if you use it daily.

        One practical note for Indian buyers: air fryers draw significant current when preheating. Plug directly into a wall socket — not into an extension board running other high-wattage appliances simultaneously.

    - id: features
      title: Features That Actually Matter for Indian Cooking
      icon: "🎛️"
      image: "https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1556909114-f6e7ad7d3136?w=800&q=80&auto=format"
      content: |
        Manufacturers add many features. Most are gimmicks for Indian cooking. These are the ones that genuinely matter.

        **Preheat function** — Essential. Indian snacks need consistent high heat from the moment they go in. An air fryer that preheats before you add food gives dramatically better results than one that heats gradually with food inside.

        **Temperature up to 200°C** — Necessary. Samosas and bread pakoras need 190-200°C for proper crispiness. Models that only go to 180°C will leave chewy, pale results.

        **Non-stick removable basket** — Non-negotiable. Indian cooking involves oil, spice residue and marinades. A removable, non-stick basket makes cleanup manageable. Fixed baskets become a maintenance nightmare within weeks.

        **Timer with auto-shutoff** — Standard on most models but worth confirming. The point of an air fryer is that you set it and walk away.

        **Rotating basket** — Nice to have for small items like makhana or peanuts. Not necessary for flat items like tikka or cutlets.

    - id: indian-food
      title: What Indian Food Works in an Air Fryer
      icon: "🍳"
      image: "https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1601050690597-df0568f70950?w=800&q=80&auto=format"
      content: |
        Understanding what works and what doesn't will set realistic expectations.

        **Works excellently:** Samosas (fresh and frozen), pakoras, bread rolls, chicken tikka, tandoori chicken, fish tikka, paneer tikka, roasted makhana, french fries, onion rings, reheating leftovers and pizza, roasting papad and namkeen, baked snacks.

        **Works reasonably well:** Cutlets and tikkis (with a light oil spray), stuffed paratha (different from tawa but decent), roasted corn, roasted vegetables.

        **Does not work:** Dal, curries, gravies, anything with significant liquid, idli, dhokla. An air fryer is fundamentally a dry-heat appliance — it cannot replace wet cooking methods.

        **Pro tip for Indian cooking:** Most Indian recipes call for deep frying at 180-190°C. Set your air fryer 10-15°C higher than the deep fry temperature, and reduce cook time by about 20%. This compensates for the absence of oil immersion.

    - id: brands
      title: Most Reliable Brands in India (2026)
      icon: "🏆"
      image: "https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1563013544-824ae1b704d3?w=800&q=80&auto=format"
      content: |
        After-sales service availability in India is critical for any appliance. Stick to brands with verified service centres.

        **Philips** — The gold standard. Most expensive but consistently the best build quality, most reliable heating element, and widest service network in India. Buy Philips if your budget allows.

        **Inalsa** — Indian brand, excellent value, solid service network. Performance is very good. The best option in the budget-to-mid segment for Indian buyers.

        **Ninja** — Excellent performance, especially the dual-zone models. Growing service presence in India in 2026. Premium priced but worth it for serious cooks.

        **Havells and Bajaj** — Reliable Indian brands with strong service networks. Mid-range performance. Good choice if after-sales support is your top priority at a mid price point.

        **Avoid:** Unknown brands with no physical service centre presence in India. The cheapest options frequently develop heating element failures within 6-12 months with no way to get them repaired.

    - id: budget
      title: How Much Should You Spend
      icon: "💰"
      image: "https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1580519542036-c47de6196ba5?w=800&q=80&auto=format"
      content: |
        Air fryer pricing in India falls into three clear tiers.

        **Under ₹3,000** — Entry-level. Small capacity, lower wattage, basic features. Fine for a single person experimenting. Not recommended for serious daily family use.

        **₹3,000 to ₹6,000** — Mid-range. The sweet spot for most Indian families. 4-6 litre capacity, 1400-1600W, good feature sets from Inalsa, Havells and Bajaj. Best value tier.

        **₹6,000 to ₹12,000** — Premium. Philips, Ninja and high-end models. Better build quality, more even cooking, longer lifespan. Worth it if you will use it daily for 5+ years.

        **Above ₹12,000** — Dual-zone or large-capacity models. Only for large families or people who entertain regularly.

        Our recommendation for most Indian families: spend ₹4,000 to ₹6,000 on an Inalsa or mid-range Philips. That covers daily use for 5+ years without overspending.
